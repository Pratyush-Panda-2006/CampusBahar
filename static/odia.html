HOSTEL TO STREET SURVIVAL GUIDE

Odia phrases so autos don't overcharge you.

Transliterated everyday phrases for non-Odia hostellers from Jharkhand, Bihar, UP, and Bengal. Speak with confidence and unlock genuine local hospitality.

Category 01

Auto & Transit Negotiation

🚌 View Mo Bus Guide ➔
"ଖଣ୍ଡଗିରି ଯିବେ କି?"
🗣️ Khandagiri jibe ki?
Meaning: "Will you go to Khandagiri?"
Tip: Look directly at the driver and speak clearly. Shared autos queue right outside ITER Gate 1.
"କେତେ ଟଙ୍କା ହେବ?"
🗣️ Kete tonka heba?
Meaning: "How much will the fare be?"
Tip: Standard shared rate from Jagamara to Khandagiri is ₹15–₹20 per seat.
"ଏଇଠି ରଖନ୍ତୁ ଭାଇ।"
🗣️ Eithi rokhantu bhai.
Meaning: "Please stop here, brother."
Tip: Say this 20 meters before reaching ITER Gate 1 or the trail entry point.
Category 02

Street Food & Refreshments

"ଦହିବରା ଆଳୁଦମ୍ ଗୋଟେ ପ୍ଲେଟ୍ ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ।"
🗣️ Dahi Bara Aloo Dum gote plate diantu.
Meaning: "Please give one plate of Dahi Bara Aloo Dum."
Tip: Bhubaneswar's holy grail snack. Always ask for complimentary spicy 'Dahi Paani' at the end!
"ଟିକେ ପାଣି ମିଳିବ କି?"
🗣️ Tike paani milibo ki?
Meaning: "Can I get some drinking water?"
Tip: Vital for hydration during midday warm climbs atop Udayagiri hill.
"ପିଆଜ ଟିକେ ବେଶୀ ଦେବେ।"
🗣️ Piaji tikke besi debe.
Meaning: "Please add a bit more chopped onions."
Tip: Works wonders at Ghugni, Chaat, and Bara stalls outside Khandagiri square.
Category 03

Campus Navigation & Courtesy

"ମୋ କଲେଜ ଆଇଟିଇଆର୍ (ITER) ଜଗମରା ରେ ଅଛି।"
🗣️ Mo college ITER Jagamara re achi.
Meaning: "My college is at ITER Jagamara."
Tip: If an auto driver is confused, mention 'SOA ITER Jagamara Gate 1' or 'SUM Hospital road'.
"ଧନ୍ୟବାଦ!"
🗣️ Dhanyabad!
Meaning: "Thank you!"
Tip: Universally appreciated by elders, street vendors, and conductors.
"ମୁଁ ବାହାର ପିଲା, ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଶିଖୁଛି।"
🗣️ Mun bahara pila, Odia sikhuchi.
Meaning: "I am from outside state, learning Odia."
Tip: Instant smile guaranteed. Odia locals are extraordinarily welcoming when you try.

Cultural Pro-Tip for Hostellers

In Odisha, calling elder auto drivers or shopkeepers "Bhai" (Brother) or "Mousa" (Uncle) with a calm tone instantly disarms any tension and establishes mutual respect. You don't need fluent grammar—speaking these simple phrases proves you respect the land you're studying in.
